Football analytics on free data

Three machine-learning projects: an expected-goals model, a player scouting tool and a Fantasy Premier League manager. Every one runs on free, permitted data sources and free hosting.

xG Lab

An expected-goals model trained on 37,881 shots from StatsBomb open data.

  • Matches StatsBomb's commercial xG within ~0.005 log loss on World Cup 2022, Euro 2024 and Copa América 2024.
  • This season's top-5 leagues and Champions League, updated daily: who is creating chances and who is riding luck.
  • xG calculator that explains why a shot is worth what it is.

Scout

Find players with a similar statistical profile: "who plays like Hakimi, but younger?"

  • 2024/25 top-5 leagues and Champions League, with role-specific per-90 profiles and cosine similarity.
  • Talent finder for U21–U25 players, head-to-head radar charts and leaderboards.
  • The 2015/16 deep dive found Kanté → Idrissa Gueye, the player Everton actually signed as his replacement.

FPL auto-manager

A Telegram bot that manages a Fantasy Premier League team, running free on GitHub Actions.

  • Two-stage points model that predicts each scoring part (minutes, goals, assists, clean sheets, bonus, defensive contributions). It retrains as the season goes on.
  • An integer-programming optimiser plans transfers with the bank, free transfers and expected price changes. A separate planner times the chips.
  • Reads a screenshot of the Transfers page, sends the report before each deadline and draws both lineups in formation.
  • Runs a "ghost team" in parallel and scores its own predictions every week.

In a deadline-by-deadline replay of 2025/26 (same optimiser, no chips), this model scored 2,231 points. Driven by FPL's own expected points instead, the bot scored 2,030.

Data: StatsBomb Open Data, the public FPL API, TheSportsDB, Highlightly and API-Football (free plans, within their terms). No betting odds are used anywhere.
